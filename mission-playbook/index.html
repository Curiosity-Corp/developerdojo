See the outcome · fictional example

A useful estimate, with its limits attached

Goal: compare two lighting schedules for a fictional community room.

Check: six 100 W lights for five hours on 30 days use 90 kWh; six 12 W lights use 10.8 kWh under the same assumptions.

Decision still needed: compare actual brightness, measured usage, costs and compatibility. A qualified person reviews real installation work.

This is an illustrative brief based on the lighting energy kata, not a completed customer mission or measured saving. The technique transfers: keep inputs and assumptions explicit, ask an assistant for a draft, and check the arithmetic independently.

Choose your approach

Five moves for a mission

  1. Agree the goal and permissions. Identify the decision-maker, audience, permitted source material, constraints and what success looks like.
  2. Adapt a kata. Pick a technique that fits. Use the context-trust kata when reading an unfamiliar source, or the run record when comparing approaches.
  3. Choose your stack. Record the harness, model, instructions, tools, versions and permissions actually used. Separate your decisions from what an agent did.
  4. Verify the result. Define observable checks, keep the source and output where authorized, and record failures or uncertainty. Arrange independent or qualified review when the work calls for it.
  5. Learn and share responsibly. Capture what changed and what evidence supports an improvement. Share a sanitized lesson only with permission and appropriate credit.

Use any assistant you can access on a phone or computer, or a trusted CLI harness. This page does not connect to a service or execute an agent. Provider access, terms and costs vary.

A worked mission · real, public files

One check, three fields

Where this comes from: Built by the Dojo maintainers from the site’s own UX, BIM and agriculture pathways. It is not a learner, client or customer mission, and it uses only files those pathways already publish with their sources and terms. No independent review has assessed it yet.

The brief

Goal
Show that one foundation technique carries into three specialist fields, and record exactly what each field changes: its reference, capabilities, standards, stack and required review. For: Learners who finished the two foundation katas and want to see a technique move between fields before they carry one themselves.

Chosen stack

Dependencies
The foundation katas PRACTICE-001 Compare AI instructions and PRACTICE-002 Check context and trust, which every kata in the three pathways names as its foundation.
Acceptance criteria and who checks them
  • SaaS UX: The Penpot file is rebuilt from the flow and tokens, and every token colour pair meets its contrast ratio. Rerun by CI on every pipeline.
  • SaaS UX: The working example is driven through every state with axe, reflow and focus checks, and the clickable prototype through every transition. Rerun by CI on every pipeline.
  • SaaS UX: The flow file is redrawn as a sitemap, a one-canvas journey and one low-fidelity screen per state, and rechecked for reachability, dead ends, error recovery, dialogs, button wiring and token contrast. Rerun by CI on every pipeline.
  • Architecture and BIM: Inspection, property sets, IDS, schema and geometry on the original and the edited copy, the 3D mesh and its checks, the traced quantities and their cross-checks, and the Randori cards. Rerun by CI on every pipeline.
  • Agriculture: FAO worked examples, the record inspection, daily and monthly reference evapotranspiration, the maize irrigation estimate and the Randori cards. Rerun by CI on every pipeline.
Independent review and qualified professional approval required

  • Architecture and BIM: required for real use. None. Nothing here has professional, regulatory or construction approval of any kind, and a real project needs the qualified reviewers its contract and jurisdiction name.
  • Agriculture: required for real use. None. Nothing here is an irrigation recommendation or has been reviewed by an agronomist, irrigation engineer or water authority; real water use needs the qualified people and local rules that apply to it.

Sanitized retrospective · checked 2026-11-21

What the mission taught, and where it goes

What actually happened
The three pathways were built separately, each on the same two foundation katas. Read side by side, each field has the same review gate in its own terms, and CI recomputes every output on every pipeline.
Evidence that supports it
The pathway pages, their example files and the outputs CI recomputes: SaaS UX README, Architecture and BIM README, Agriculture README.
Failures and what changed
  • SaaS UX: no failure is recorded in its kata recipes.
  • BIM-001: The opening has no container of its own in the storey's contained elements. That is correct. An opening belongs to the wall it voids (IfcRelVoidsElement); the script reports its container through the wall.
  • BIM-002: A script prints FireRating as a value, so it looks filled in. The value is an empty IfcLabel. Print the raw value and its type, and treat an empty string as missing.
  • BIM-003: IfcTester says the property set does not contain FireRating, but the file has it. The property exists with an empty IfcLabel. IfcTester 0.9.0 treats an empty value as missing, which is what REQ-03 needs to catch.
  • BIM-004: The edited file differs from the original on almost every line. IfcOpenShell rewrites the file in its own layout and drops comments. Compare entities and properties, not text lines; change.json is that comparison.
  • BIM-005: The volumes are a billion times too big. The sample's length unit is the millimetre. Multiply each coordinate by the unit scale (0.001) before you measure.
  • BIM-005: Edges are used by one triangle only, so the mesh looks open. Some exporters repeat a vertex for each face. Weld vertices with the same coordinates before you count edges.
  • BIM-006: The wall volume is 1,800,000,000. That is cubic millimetres. Convert each length to metres first, using the model's length unit (millimetre here).
  • BIM-006: The net face area is 4 m², not 5 m². The opening was deducted from both faces and the result added up as one face. The opening passes right through, so deduct it once from each face and report one face.
  • BIM-R01: Card C compared with the wrong unit. IDS values are in SI units. The sample declares no thermal transmittance unit, so its value is already in W/(m2 K).
  • AGR-001: A trace of rain is recorded as 0 mm. That is how NOAA records it; the T flag says some rain fell but too little to measure. Count trace days separately; do not treat them as missing.
  • AGR-002: Example 8 gives the wrong Ra. The latitude goes in radians and is negative in the southern hemisphere; Example 8 is at 20 degrees south.
  • AGR-003: Effective rain is zero in May although 8.9 mm fell. The rule gives 0.6 × 8.9 − 10, which is below zero, so it is floored at zero. Small monthly rain is assumed lost to evaporation and runoff.
  • AGR-R01: Card C was expected to lower the need a lot because the season moves later. The season also ends a month later, in September, so the hot months stay inside it; only the total over the shifted days changes.
What remains uncertain or untested
No learner has run any of the three pathways, no independent reviewer has assessed the transfer, and nothing here has the professional review that real BIM or agriculture work requires. Each pathway lists what it did not verify: SaaS UX (7 items), Architecture and BIM (9 items), Agriculture (5 items).

Your turn

Make a brief you own

The template has three parts: the mission brief, the transfer record and the retrospective. Edit it on your device. The Dojo does not save or send what you type. If you copy or share it with another service, that service receives it; include only information you are permitted to share.

What each part asks for

Mission brief

Goal, audience and decision-maker
What result, for whom, and who decides that it is done. Use identifiers your agreement allows.
Authorized inputs and permissions
Each input, where it came from, its licence or terms, and who allowed you to use it. Leave out anything you may not share.
Real-world constraints
Time, budget, data, privacy, the tools you may or may not use, and the limits set by the field or the people involved.
Chosen stack
The harness, model, instructions, tools, versions and permissions you actually used, and why this stack.
Dependencies
The people, systems, files and earlier work the mission needs, and what happens if one is missing.
Acceptance criteria and who checks them
Observable checks written before the work starts, and who runs each one.
Independent review and qualified professional approval required
Who must review or approve the result before anyone relies on it. Write "none required" only with the reason.
Katas practiced and how I will adapt them
The kata IDs you practiced and the technique you are taking from each.

Transfer record

Foundation technique that transfers
The technique in one sentence, and the foundation kata it comes from.
Specialist capabilities and standards that change
For the new field: its reference or source of truth, the capabilities, standards and tools it adds, and the review it requires.
Independent rubric result
The criterion ID from the assessment map (MD-1 or SEN-2), recorded as Observed, Not yet observed or Unclear with an evidence reference, by an independent reviewer. Write Unavailable until a reviewer has recorded it.

Retrospective

What actually happened
The result and the main decisions, as they happened rather than as planned.
Evidence that supports it
Where each artifact, check result and log can be reviewed, privately if it has to be.
Failures and what changed
What went wrong, what caused it, what you changed and which check confirmed the repair.
What remains uncertain or untested
Checks that did not run, measurements that are unavailable, and reviews still owed.
A verified technique improvement or benchmark correction
Only one you reproduced and can show evidence for, with nothing private in it.
Where I am sending it
A technique improvement or a benchmark correction goes to the Sensei contribution process with its worksheet.
What may be shared, by whom, under which licences
What you removed to make it shareable, and who agreed to the rest being shared.

Where a verified improvement goes

  • Technique improvement: A change to a kata step or technique that you reproduced and can show evidence for. Use the technique improvement worksheet and the Sensei contribution guide.
  • Benchmark correction: A dated benchmark or evidence claim on this site that your own reproduced run shows is wrong or out of date. Use the benchmark correction worksheet and the Sensei contribution guide.
  • A brief, a transfer record or a retrospective is a planning and learning aid. It is not proof of completed work and does not award a belt or a rank.
  • The transfer criteria come from the assessment map and are proposed. Only an independent reviewer records a result; until then it is unavailable.
  • Regulated or safety-critical work needs approval from a qualified professional, separate from any Dojo review.
  • This site does not save or send what you type. Share a retrospective only with permission, and without client, customer or personal material.
