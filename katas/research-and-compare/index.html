Verify and hand off

Make the decision reviewable

  1. Source of truth: identify the approved baseline and version, who owns it and where it is stored. This kata’s S1 and S2 are fictional text inside the prompt, not real documents or links.
  2. Trace every difference: compare each proposed row with S1, record the source line and label its status. S2 suggests optional workshops; it does not authorize mandatory ones.
  3. Separate phases: preserve the original version and its history; document migration completeness before proposing redesign. Do not assume a copied draft is an approved successor.
  4. Test an uncertainty: hypothesis: “An optional workshop improves completion.” No completion data is supplied. Define the population, comparison, measure, permission and a fair follow-up before drawing a conclusion.
  5. Approval and rollback: identify the actual decision-maker and request a review of each change. If approved, retain the prior version and the documented restoration steps; verify a restored copy before relying on rollback. This practice grants no approval.
Quick check: what may phase 1 change?

None of the approved A–C wording in this example. Preserve the baseline and its history; put B, C and D in a separate proposal for a decision.

For real work, obtain permission before sharing source material with an assistant. Do not paste private personnel, client or confidential project details into an unapproved service. Standards and regulated decisions require the appropriate qualified review.
