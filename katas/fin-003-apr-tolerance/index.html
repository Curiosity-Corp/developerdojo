Specialization kata · FIN-003

Check an annual percentage rate against the Regulation Z tolerance

Check an annual percentage rate against the tolerance US Regulation Z allows: read the tolerance from the regulation’s text, work out which disclosed rates count as accurate, and see what a common shortcut, ignoring the odd days before the first payment, does to the rate.

The outcome

For each Appendix J example, the range of disclosed annual percentage rates that counts as accurate, and whether the odd-days shortcut stays inside it.

For: Learners who finished FIN-002 and want to know how precise a disclosed rate has to be, and why.

Done when

  • You quote the tolerance sentence of 1026.22(a)(2) and say why it, not (a)(3), applies to every example
  • Your accurate ranges match /examples/finance-pathway/generated/tolerance.json
  • You name the examples where ignoring the odd days moves the rate beyond the tolerance

You make: Tolerance check. Outcome: Check apr tolerance.

Showcase and provenance

The Dojo’s worked example, made by the maintainers rather than by a learner: US Regulation Z’s own worked examples reproduced number for number, one of them amortized to the cent with its disclosure figures, and every annual percentage rate checked against the tolerance read from the regulation’s text, all computed by the site’s CI.

Stack recipes

  • Python standard library

Why this stack

The standard library is enough for these formulas, runs offline with nothing to install, and gives the same numbers on every machine. CI runs the identical script on every pipeline.

Alternatives

  • A spreadsheet's RATE function or an online loan calculator. Faster for a regular loan, but neither counts an odd first period by Appendix J's rules, and a calculator hides its method. Not tried for this pathway, so their numbers are not compared here. Not tested for this recipe.

Practice exercise

Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.22. From Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.22: determination of annual percentage rate (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau).

Terms: A US government work, not under copyright in the US (17 U.S.C. 105). Free to read and reuse.

Execution and handoffs

  1. Learner: Read 1026.22(a)(1) to (a)(3) and decide which tolerance applies to each Appendix J example.
  2. Explaining assistant: Drafts the code that reads the tolerance from the text and works out each range. Hand-off: You run it and compare the ranges with the CI output.
  3. Learner: Recompute each rate with t = 1 and f = 0 and compare it with the range.

Failures and corrections

  • Observed: You apply the 1/4-point tolerance to example (c)(1)(ii) because its first period is long Correction: 1026.22(a)(3) says an irregular first period, or an irregular first or final payment, does not make a transaction irregular; the 1/8-point tolerance applies.

Adapt it

  • Round the exact rate to two decimals before applying the tolerance A range edge can move by a hundredth; the script applies the tolerance to the unrounded rate.

Belt evidence

Belt stages are proposed practice stages, not issued credentials. Evidence below is what a reviewer would look for; it is not a record that anyone has shown it.

Evidence a reviewer looks for

  • For each Appendix J example, the tolerance that applies, the lowest and highest disclosed rate that counts as accurate, and the rate with the odd days ignored, matching /examples/finance-pathway/generated/tolerance.json (Automated check)
  • The tolerance is quoted from 12 CFR 1026.22(a) with its paragraph, not typed from memory (Self-check)

Belt criteria

  • Practice: Reads the tolerance from 1026.22(a)(2), works out the accurate range for each example, and finds the examples where ignoring the odd days moves the rate beyond it
  • Adapt: Explains from 1026.22(a)(3) why an odd first period or an odd final payment does not make a transaction irregular, and which features would
  • Demonstrate: Explains to a reviewer why a rate inside the tolerance on these examples says nothing about whether a real disclosure is accurate

Standards in scope

  • Regulation Z (Truth in Lending), 12 CFR part 1026, Appendix J and 1026.22, eCFR for 2026-11-23; Appendix J and 1026.22 unchanged there since 2017-01-01. Applies to: The accuracy tolerances of 1026.22(a)(2) and (a)(3), read from the served text, applied to Appendix J’s own worked examples for practice. Review scope: What the listed checks test: that the tolerance read and the ranges computed match the CI output. No claim about any real disclosure.

Maintenance and caveats

Review cadence: Every six months, or when the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau amends Appendix J or 1026.22

  • This recipe is a draft: the example was built and checked by the Dojo maintainers and CI, and no learner has run it yet.
  • This is practice on the regulation’s own examples, not a compliance review. No compliance specialist, lawyer or accountant has reviewed it, and a real loan disclosure needs the review its lender’s compliance function and counsel require.
  • Appendix J’s examples use dates in 1978 and are illustrative; they are not a current rate or offer.
  • The mortgage tolerances of 1026.22(a)(4) and (a)(5), and the finance-charge tolerances they rely on, are not covered.
