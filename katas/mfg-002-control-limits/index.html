Specialization kata · MFG-002

Set Shewhart control limits, with factors you computed yourself, and choose the subgroup

Rebuild the handbook’s X-bar and R factor table from the distribution of the range of normal samples, reproduce its individuals chart example, then set X-bar and s limits on the lithography record with the wafer as subgroup and see why the cassette means need limits of their own.

The outcome

The factor table rebuilt from first principles, then control limits for the lithography record that you can defend for the subgroup you chose.

For: Learners who finished MFG-001 and want to compute control limits rather than take them from a template.

Done when

  • Your A2, D3 and D4 match the handbook’s table for n = 2 to 10, with the one D4 the reference checks note
  • Your X-bar and s limits for the wafer subgroups and your individuals limits for the cassette means match /examples/manufacturing-pathway/generated/control-limits.json

You make: Control chart limits. Outcome: Set control limits.

Showcase and provenance

The Dojo’s worked example, made by the maintainers rather than by a learner: the NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook’s lithography case study reproduced number for number, its control chart factors rebuilt from the distribution of the range, control limits set with two different subgroups, and the line-width variation split into cassette, wafer and site, all computed by the site’s CI.

Stack recipes

  • Python standard library

Why this stack

The standard library is enough for these formulas, runs offline with nothing to install, and gives the same numbers on every machine. CI runs the identical script on every pipeline.

Alternatives

  • A statistics package or a spreadsheet control chart template. Faster to draw the chart, but a template hides which sigma it uses and which subgroup it assumes, the two choices this pathway is about. Not tried for this pathway, so their numbers are not compared here. Not tested for this recipe.

Agents and review gates

  1. Explaining assistant

    Explains d2, d3 and c4 and drafts Python for the integrals and the limits.

    Review gate: You run the code and keep it only if it reproduces the handbook’s printed values.

  2. Learner as reviewer

    Works out one limit by hand from s-bar and c4 and compares every factor and limit with the handbook and the CI output.

    Depends on: Explaining assistant

    Review gate: A factor stays only if it matches the handbook’s table at three decimals, or within the one noted unit.

Setup and smoke test

  2. Open handbook sections 6.3.2, 6.3.2.1, 6.3.2.2 and 6.6.1.4 beside the script.

Sign-in and access: None. Nothing here needs an account.

Execution and handoffs

  1. Explaining assistant: Drafts the code for d2, d3, the factors and c4. Hand-off: You run it and compare every factor with section 6.3.2.1.
  2. Learner: Reproduce the individuals chart example, then set the X-bar and s limits with the wafer as subgroup and count the means outside them.
  3. Learner: Chart the 30 cassette means as individuals and compare with section 6.6.1.4.

Failures and corrections

  • Observed: Most wafer means fall outside the X-bar limits, so the process looks badly out of control Correction: The limits carry only the variation within a wafer, while the means also move from cassette to cassette. Section 6.6.1.4 charts the lot means with limits from the lot-to-lot variation instead.
  • Observed: The s chart has a negative lower limit Correction: For n = 5 the lower limit is below zero, so it is set to zero; the handbook notes there is no lower limit for this subgroup size.

Adapt it

  • Use the cassette (15 measurements) as the subgroup The limits narrow; the Randori card B asks whether fewer means fall outside them and why.

Belt evidence

Belt stages are proposed practice stages, not issued credentials. Evidence below is what a reviewer would look for; it is not a record that anyone has shown it.

Evidence a reviewer looks for

  • The 27 factors of the X-bar and R table and the individuals chart example matching sections 6.3.2.1 and 6.3.2.2, and the limits for the lithography record, as the CI output does (Automated check)
  • The learner states which subgroup each chart uses and what variation its limits carry (Self-check)

Belt criteria

  • Practice: Reproduces A2, D3 and D4 for n = 2 to 10 and the individuals chart example of section 6.3.2.2
  • Adapt: Sets X-bar and s limits on the lithography record with the wafer as subgroup and explains why so many wafer means fall outside them
  • Demonstrate: Explains to a reviewer why the cassette means need limits from the lot-to-lot variation, as section 6.6.1.4 says, and what the individuals chart on them shows

Maintenance and caveats

Review cadence: Every six months, or when NIST changes the handbook’s chapter 6 or the case study’s data file

  • This recipe is a draft: the example was built and checked by the Dojo maintainers and CI, and no learner has run it yet.
  • This is practice on the handbook’s own case study, not an engineering review. No quality engineer, statistician or process engineer has reviewed it, and a real process needs the review its quality system requires.
  • The served data are the 450 rows of LITHOGRA.DAT without its header, which names the person who supplied the data.
  • Section 6.6.1.4 recommends charting the lot means with lot-to-lot limits; the individuals chart on the cassette means is one standard way to do that, chosen by the Dojo.
