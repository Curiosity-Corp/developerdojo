Science · Pathway

From a published record to a result with its uncertainty.

Four katas on a real research record and the international guide to measurement uncertainty. Each step has a file you can open and a result that CI computes again on every pipeline.

Start with SCI-001Download the record

01 / The files

One record, one method, every number rerun

The record is NASA’s GISTEMP v4 table of global land and ocean temperature anomalies: 147 years, 1880 to 2026, a value for each month, the annual mean (J-D) and seasonal means, in degrees Celsius against the 1951-1980 mean. The method is the GUM, the international guide to measurement uncertainty: the script reproduces its worked examples before it touches the record.

  • The temperature record (CSV, as NASA served it), unchanged.
  • The uncertainty budget (CSV) and as JSON.
  • The fit report (JSON) and its residuals (CSV).
  • The result summary (Markdown) and every result under the manifest, with the method and the input hash.
  • The pathway script (Python 3.12, standard library only, MIT), so you can rerun every number yourself.
  • README: sources, terms, how to rerun the checks, and what they do not cover.

02 / What is in the record

147 years, 7 cells unavailable

A cell of asterisks means GISS has no value yet. The script reads it as unavailable, leaves it out of every sum and never treats it as zero. 146 years have all twelve months.

Monthly values run from -0.81 °C (Jan 1893) to 1.55 °C (Oct 2026); annual means from -0.48 °C (1909) to 1.28 °C (2024). Over the base period 1951-1980 the annual means average 0 °C, as an anomaly from that period should. Of 146 complete years, the published J-D equals the mean of its twelve months at two decimals in 134, and differs by no more than 0.01 °C, the printed precision, in 146. Full inspection (JSON).

03 / The steps

Four katas, in order

Each kata has a brief, prompts, checks and the evidence for the checks the Dojo has run. They are drafts: the Dojo built the example and CI checks it, and no learner has run them yet. Do the foundations first: Compare AI instructions and Spot a context trap.

  1. SCI-001 Inspect a published research record

    Years, columns, unit, the missing-value marker and whether the annual means agree with their months, before any number is used.

  2. SCI-002 Evaluate measurement uncertainty

    Reproduce the GUM’s worked example: Type A standard uncertainties, correlated inputs and an uncertainty budget.

  3. SCI-003 Fit a line and report its uncertainty

    Reproduce the GUM’s calibration line, then fit 30 years of the record and say what the uncertainty does and does not cover.

  4. SCI-R01 Science Randori: an unseen change

    A partner reveals one changed assumption after you start; predict its effect on the slope and its uncertainty, then recompute.

The standard uncertainties assume independent residuals, as the least-squares formulas do. The residual lag-1 autocorrelation is reported so you can see whether that holds; this is not the uncertainty of the record. GISS states the uncertainty of its annual global means separately (about ±0.05 °C after 1960); this pathway does not evaluate it. A line over these years describes these years; it is not a forecast. 05 / The tools and sources

No packages, public sources

The script uses only the Python 3.12 standard library, so it runs offline with nothing to install. A spreadsheet works for checking one row by hand. The Armory lists the stack recipe Python standard library.

  • JCGM 100:2008, Guide to the expression of uncertainty in measurement (GUM): Type A evaluation (clause 4.2), correlated inputs (clause 5.2) and the worked examples in Annex H.2 and H.3. Only its published numbers are used, as checks; no text is copied.
  • NIST Technical Note 1297: NIST’s statement of the same method.
  • NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods, section 1.3.5.12: the autocorrelation coefficient used on the residuals.
  • NASA GISS Surface Temperature Analysis (GISTEMP) v4: the record, its FAQ and the analysis paper, doi:10.1029/2023JD040179. GISTEMP combines NOAA’s GHCN v4 land stations and ERSST v5 sea surface temperatures.

06 / What is checked

Verified, and not verified

Verified

  • The temperature record is the file NASA served on the date retrieved, unchanged (SHA-256 a7b1939db2e7…, checked by the site’s tests).
  • The script reproduces 55 published values from the GUM’s worked examples (Annex H.2, Tables H.2 to H.5, and Annex H.3, Table H.6) and refuses to write any result if one differs. (evidence)
  • The record has 147 years and 7 unavailable cells, each read as unavailable and never as zero; in 134 of 146 complete years the published annual mean equals the mean of its months at two decimals. (evidence)
  • On every pipeline, CI reruns the script with Python 3.12 and fails if any output differs from the files on this page. (evidence)

Not verified

  • The uncertainty of the record itself. GISS evaluates it separately; this pathway neither reproduces nor checks it.
  • Whether the residuals of the line are independent. Their lag-1 autocorrelation is reported (0.19 for the annual fit, 0.61 for the monthly one on Randori card B), but no test is run and no autocorrelation-corrected uncertainty is computed.
  • Any real measurement, instrument or calibration. The GUM’s Annex H data are illustrative, and nothing here is a calibration certificate or a statement of traceability.
  • GISTEMP’s own method: the station and sea-surface processing behind the record is not rerun.
  • Use by real learners: no learner has run these katas yet.

Professional review: None. No metrologist, statistician or climate scientist has reviewed this pathway; a real measurement result, calibration or published finding needs the qualified reviewers its laboratory, journal or accreditation body names.
