Specialization kata · MED-003

Normalise to EBU R 128 and check the true-peak limit

Work out the gain that brings the NASA recording to the EBU R 128 target of -23.0 LUFS, check what that gain does to its true peak against the -1 dBTP limit, remeasure after the gain, and say when a gain alone is not enough.

The outcome

A normalisation plan for the recording, a gain and its effect on loudness and true peak, that a reviewer can check line by line.

For: Learners who finished MED-002 and deliver audio or video to a loudness target.

Done when

  • Your gain, remeasured loudness and true peak after the gain match /examples/media-pathway/generated/normalisation.json
  • You state whether the gain alone meets the target and the true-peak limit, and the largest gain the limit allows

You make: Normalisation plan. Outcome: Normalise loudness.

Showcase and provenance

The Dojo’s worked example, made by the maintainers rather than by a learner: a loudness meter written from ITU-R BS.1770-5, checked against EBU Tech 3341’s published test responses, then used on NASA’s Artemis II launch recording to measure its loudness and true peak and work out its EBU R 128 normalisation, all computed by the site’s CI.

Stack recipes

Why this stack

The standard library reads WAV files and is enough for these filters, runs offline with nothing to install, and gives the same numbers on every machine. CI runs the identical script on every pipeline.

Alternatives

  • A loudness meter in a digital audio workstation or a command-line audio tool. Faster on long programmes, but the meter is a closed box unless you check it with the Tech 3341 test signals yourself, which is the habit this pathway teaches. Not tried for this pathway, so their numbers are not compared here. Not tested for this recipe.

Agents and review gates

  1. Explaining assistant

    Explains loudness normalisation and the true-peak limit and drafts the code that applies a gain and remeasures.

    Review gate: You run the code and keep it only if the remeasured loudness lands where the gain says it should.

  2. Learner as reviewer

    Checks that the gain in dB moves the loudness and the true peak by the same amount, and compares every value with the CI output.

    Depends on: Explaining assistant

    Review gate: A value stays only if it matches the CI output at the precision shown.

Setup and smoke test

  1. Use the folder, Python and meter from MED-002.
  2. Download EBU R 128 from the EBU and open it beside the script.

Sign-in and access: None. Nothing here needs an account.

Execution and handoffs

  1. Explaining assistant: Drafts the gain calculation and the code that applies it and remeasures. Hand-off: You run the meter on the gained recording and compare.
  2. Learner: Compute the gain to -23.0 LUFS, remeasure, and check the true peak after the gain against -1 dBTP.
  3. Learner: Work out the largest gain the true-peak limit allows and the loudness it would give.

Failures and corrections

  • Observed: The remeasured loudness is not exactly the measured loudness plus the gain Correction: A gain moves every block by the same amount, so the same blocks pass the gates, but the gain is applied to the samples and the meter runs again in floating point. A difference in the second decimal is rounding; more than that means the gain was applied in the wrong unit.
  • Observed: The gain to the target would put the true peak above -1 dBTP Correction: A gain alone cannot meet both. The programme needs its peaks reduced first, for example with a true-peak limiter, which this pathway does not cover; never clip the samples to fit.

Adapt it

  • Plan the normalisation of the Tech 3341 case 19 tone (true peak +3 dBTP while its samples stay just below full scale) Its sample peak hides an inter-sample peak of about 3 dB, so a ceiling set from the sample peak would let it through.

Belt evidence

Belt stages are proposed practice stages, not issued credentials. Evidence below is what a reviewer would look for; it is not a record that anyone has shown it.

Evidence a reviewer looks for

  • The gain to the target, the loudness remeasured after the gain, the true peak after the gain and the gain the true-peak limit allows, matching the CI output (Automated check)
  • The learner states what they would do if the gain to the target pushed the true peak over -1 dBTP (Self-check)

Belt criteria

  • Practice: Computes the gain to -23.0 LUFS and remeasures the recording after applying it
  • Adapt: Checks the true peak after the gain against -1 dBTP and works out the largest gain the limit allows
  • Demonstrate: Explains to a reviewer when a gain alone meets R 128 and when the programme needs its peaks reduced first, without claiming any delivery is compliant

Standards in scope

  • EBU R 128, Loudness normalisation and permitted maximum level of audio signals, V5 (November 2023); read 2026-11-25. Applies to: The target level of -23.0 LUFS, its tolerance of +/-0.5 LU for programmes that are not live, and the maximum true peak of -1 dBTP, applied to the NASA recording for practice. Review scope: What the listed checks test: that the gain, the remeasured loudness and the true peak after the gain match the CI output. No claim that any programme is fit for broadcast or meets a broadcaster’s delivery specification.

Maintenance and caveats

Review cadence: Every six months, or when the ITU or the EBU revise BS.1770, Tech 3341 or R 128

  • This recipe is a draft: the example was built and checked by the Dojo maintainers and CI, and no learner has run it yet.
  • This is practice on NASA’s public recording, not a broadcast compliance check. No broadcast audio engineer or delivery quality-control specialist has reviewed it, and a real delivery needs the checks its broadcaster or platform asks for.
  • Broadcasters and platforms publish their own delivery targets; this pathway uses only the R 128 values it read and does not list others.
