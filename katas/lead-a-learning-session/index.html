Plan the session

The finished outcome

A session plan—not a claim that it happened

1 · Show
One source and one claim to check.

2 · Practice
The learner finds what the source does and does not say.

3 · Adapt
Try the same thinking with a new example.

4 · Follow through
Ask for feedback and record only what actually happens.

Goal: after practicing, the learner can identify a supported fact, an unknown and a useful next question. Status: not yet run; participant and time are not scheduled.

Ingredients: the fictional note below, the session plan, somewhere to save your notes and a willing peer when you choose to invite one. You can prepare on paper, use an assistant in a phone or browser app, or use a trusted CLI harness. Prepare the eight-minute lesson

Fictional practice note: On 10 May, two volunteers prepared six kits. One kit is still waiting for inspection. The note does not say how many kits passed inspection.

Claim to check: All six kits passed inspection.

  1. Show, one minute: share the note and the claim. Ask the learner to mark a source fact and an unknown before you explain.
  2. Practice, three minutes: ask them to explain their answer and point to the exact words in the note. Invite a question they would ask before accepting the claim.
  3. Adapt, two minutes: try a fresh fictional statement: “Eight meals were plated; two were delivered.” Ask whether it proves all eight were delivered and how they know.
  4. Reflect, two minutes: ask “What was useful?” and “What should I change next time?” Agree on a next step if the learner wants one.
Reveal the independent check

The source supports that six kits were prepared and one is waiting for inspection. It does not say how many passed, so “all six passed” is unsupported. A useful question is “How many kits passed inspection?” In the adapted example, two meals were delivered; it does not prove all eight were delivered.

Lead and learn

Invite, listen and record the real result

Offer a short session and agree on a time and format that works for both people. If it happens, invite the learner to demonstrate the method and adapt it; let them ask questions. Give feedback about the work and evidence, not assumptions about the person. Invite their feedback on your teaching.

Session record — fill in only after a real session

  • Date and participant, if they agree to be named: not recorded
  • What the learner actually demonstrated or asked: not recorded
  • Feedback they chose to share: not recorded
  • Agreed next step, owner and date if set: not recorded

If no session happens, leave it marked “not run.” Do not invent attendance, approval, learning or a next meeting. Keep real learner notes in an appropriate private place and ask permission before sharing or putting them into an AI service.
