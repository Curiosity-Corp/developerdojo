Agriculture · Pathway

From a weather record to a checked estimate.

Four katas on one year of real daily weather. Each step has a file you can open and a result that CI computes again on every pipeline.

Start with AGR-001Download the record

01 / The files

One station, one year, one crop

The record is NOAA’s daily summary for weather station USW00093193 (Fresno Yosemite International, California; 36.77999, -119.72016, 101.9 m) from 2023-01-01 to 2023-12-31: maximum and minimum temperature and rain, in metric units, with NOAA’s flags on every value. The crop is maize for grain, planted on 2023-04-01, with FAO’s general crop values. Nothing was grown; this is a practice estimate.

  • The weather record (CSV, as NOAA’s data service returned it), unchanged.
  • Daily results (CSV): radiation, Penman-Monteith and Hargreaves reference evapotranspiration for every day.
  • The result summary (Markdown) and every result as JSON under the manifest, with the method and the input hash.
  • The pathway script (Python 3.12, standard library only, MIT), so you can rerun every number yourself.
  • README: sources, terms, how to rerun the checks, and what they do not cover.

03 / The steps

Four katas, in order

Each kata has a brief, prompts, checks and the evidence for the checks the Dojo has run. They are drafts: the Dojo built the example and CI checks it, and no learner has run them yet. Do the foundations first: Compare AI instructions and Spot a context trap.

  1. AGR-001 Inspect a daily weather record

    Station, dates, units, missing values and every flag, before any number is used.

  2. AGR-002 Estimate reference evapotranspiration

    Reproduce FAO’s worked examples, then compute daily ET0 from temperature alone and compare two methods.

  3. AGR-003 Estimate a crop’s irrigation need

    Maize crop coefficients by growth stage, effective rain, and the net irrigation need by month.

  4. AGR-R01 Agriculture Randori: an unseen change

    A partner reveals one changed assumption after you start; predict its effect, then recompute.

Reference evapotranspiration (AGR-002)

FAO-56 Penman-Monteith (eq. 6), daily, with the chapter 3 estimates for missing data. Humidity: dewpoint = Tmin - 0.0 degrees C (eq. 48). Radiation: Hargreaves radiation formula, kRs = 0.16 (interior), limited to clear-sky Rso (eqs. 50, 37). Wind: u2 = 2.0 m/s (no wind in the record). The Hargreaves equation is a second, simpler estimate; where the two disagree, neither is measured truth.

05 / The tools and sources

No packages, public sources

The script uses only the Python 3.12 standard library, so it runs offline with nothing to install. A spreadsheet works for checking single days by hand. The Armory lists the stack recipe Python standard library.

  • FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56, Crop evapotranspiration (FAO, 1998): the Penman-Monteith method, the missing-data estimates, Hargreaves, crop coefficients, and Examples 8 and 18. The equations and table values are used; no text is copied.
  • FAO Irrigation Water Management Training Manual 3, Irrigation Water Needs, section 4.2: the effective-rainfall formula and its worked question.
  • NOAA NCEI GHCN-Daily: dataset metadata and terms, cited as GHCN-Daily version 3, doi:10.7289/V5D21VHZ, with the dataset overview paper doi:10.1175/JTECH-D-11-00103.1.

06 / What is checked

Verified, and not verified

Verified

  • The weather record is the file NOAA’s data service returned for the query on this page, unchanged (SHA-256 abde49906522…, checked by the site’s tests).
  • The script reproduces 13 published values from FAO’s own worked examples (FAO-56 Examples 8 and 18, and the effective-rainfall answers in FAO Training Manual 3) and refuses to write any result if one differs. (evidence)
  • The record has 365 days, 0 missing temperature or rain values and 0 quality flags, as the inspection counts them. (evidence)
  • On every pipeline, CI reruns the script with Python 3.12 and fails if any output differs from the files on this page. (evidence)

Not verified

  • Measured evapotranspiration. Humidity, radiation and wind are estimated from temperature as FAO-56 describes for missing data; no station that measures them was compared.
  • Any real field, soil, irrigation system or crop. The crop coefficients and stage lengths are FAO’s general table values, not adjusted for this climate, and nothing here was grown.
  • Soil water balance, irrigation efficiency, leaching, salinity and rainfall runoff: the estimate is the net crop water need only.
  • Whether the station record is homogeneous: NOAA says GHCN-Daily is not homogenized and was not managed to climate-monitoring standards.
  • Use by real learners: no learner has run these katas yet.

Professional review: None. Nothing here is an irrigation recommendation or has been reviewed by an agronomist, irrigation engineer or water authority; real water use needs the qualified people and local rules that apply to it.

The field template

What this field adds to the shared foundations

Every sector pathway follows the same template: the field's own terms and procedures, the standards its work is read against, the files it produces, belt criteria, and the qualified review a real use needs. The katas build on the shared foundations and do not repeat them.

Terms of the field

Reference evapotranspiration (ET0)
The water a hypothetical well-watered grass surface would lose to evaporation and transpiration, in millimetres per day, as FAO-56 defines it.
Crop coefficient (Kc)
The ratio of a crop’s evapotranspiration to ET0 at a growth stage; FAO-56 tabulates typical values.
Effective rainfall
The part of the rain that the crop can use, after runoff and deep percolation.
Net irrigation need
Crop water need minus effective rainfall, before any losses in the irrigation system.
Quality flag
A mark in a GHCN-Daily record that a value failed one of NOAA’s quality checks.

Procedures practised

  1. Inspect a station weather record: coverage, missing values and quality flags (AGR-001)
  2. Estimate reference evapotranspiration with FAO Penman-Monteith and its missing-data methods (AGR-002)
  3. Estimate a crop’s net irrigation need by growth stage (AGR-003)

Standards cited, by version

  • FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56 (Crop evapotranspiration), 1998 (read against in AGR-002, AGR-003)
  • FAO Training Manual 3 (Irrigation Water Needs), 1986 (read against in AGR-003)
