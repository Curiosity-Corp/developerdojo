Media · Pathway

From a recording to a measured, normalised programme loudness.

Four katas on audio loudness, on NASA’s public-domain launch recording and the methods of ITU-R BS.1770-5, EBU Tech 3341 and EBU R 128. Each step has a file you can open and a result that CI computes again on every pipeline.

Start with MED-001Download the loudness series

01 / The files

One recording, a meter checked first, every number rerun

The recording is NASA’s audio of the Artemis II launch from a camera site at the pad: 32.966 seconds of stereo at 48000 Hz. Recommendation ITU-R BS.1770-5 defines how to measure its loudness and true peak, EBU Tech 3341 publishes test signals with the reading a meter must give for each, and EBU R 128 sets the target. The script checks its meter against those test signals before it measures anything else.

  • The recording (WAV, 6.3 MB): NASA’s file, unchanged.
  • Momentary and short-term loudness every 100 ms (CSV) and the loudness readings (JSON).
  • The recording inspection and the normalisation plan (JSON).
  • The result summary (Markdown) and every result under the manifest, with the method and the input hash.
  • The pathway script (Python 3.12, standard library only, MIT), so you can rerun every number yourself.
  • README: sources, terms, how to rerun the checks, and what they do not cover.

02 / The steps

Four katas, in order

Each kata has a brief, prompts, checks and the evidence for the checks the Dojo has run. They are drafts: the Dojo built the example and CI checks it, and no learner has run them yet. Do the foundations first: Compare AI instructions and Spot a context trap.

  1. MED-001 Inspect a recording

    Read the WAV header, then measure each channel’s sample peak, RMS level and DC offset, and notice that the two channels are the same.

  2. MED-002 Measure loudness and true peak

    Build a meter from ITU-R BS.1770-5, check it against the EBU Tech 3341 test signals, then measure the recording.

  3. MED-003 Normalise to EBU R 128

    Work out the gain to -23.0 LUFS, remeasure, and check the true peak after the gain against -1 dBTP.

  4. MED-R01 Media Randori: an unseen change

    A partner reveals one changed choice after you start; predict how the loudness moves, then recompute.

The recording (MED-001)

2 channels, 48000 Hz, 16-bit PCM, 1582381 frames. Each channel peaks at -10.99 dBFS with an RMS level of -32.32 dBFS, and no sample reaches full scale. The two channels are identical: this is dual mono, which matters in the Randori. Result (JSON).

Normalisation to EBU R 128 (MED-003)

R 128 asks for -23.0 LUFS, within ±0.5 LU for a programme that is not live, and a true peak no higher than -1.0 dBTP. As delivered, the recording is outside that tolerance. A gain of +4.92 dB brings it to -23.02 LUFS, remeasured after the gain, with its true peak at -6.06 dBTP: within the limit, so a gain alone is enough here. The limit would allow up to 9.98 dB. Result (JSON).

04 / The tools and sources

No packages, public sources

The script uses only the Python 3.12 standard library, so it runs offline with nothing to install. Any audio player plays the recording, and a spreadsheet opens the loudness series. The Armory lists the stack recipe Python standard library.

  • Recommendation ITU-R BS.1770-5 (11/2023), Algorithms to measure audio programme loudness and true-peak audio level: the K-weighting coefficients, channel weights, gating and the true-peak annex. A free download from the ITU.
  • EBU Tech 3341 (V4, November 2023), Loudness metering, ‘EBU Mode’ metering: the momentary and short-term windows and the minimum-requirements test signals.
  • EBU R 128 (V5, November 2023), Loudness normalisation and permitted maximum level of audio signals: the target and the true-peak limit.
  • The recording on Wikimedia Commons, released by NASA Kennedy Space Center.

05 / What is checked

Verified, and not verified

Verified

  • The recording is NASA’s file, unchanged (SHA-256 18b1c6c1dae3…, checked by the site’s tests).
  • Before it measures the recording, the script synthesizes the EBU Tech 3341 test signals defined by their tones and gives 59 published test responses within their published tolerances (loudness cases 1 to 6, 9, 10, 12 and 13; true-peak cases 15 to 19), and refuses to write any result if one falls outside. (evidence)
  • The recording measures -27.92 LUFS integrated with a true peak of -10.98 dBTP; a gain of 4.92 dB brings it to -23.02 LUFS, remeasured, with its true peak at -6.06 dBTP. (evidence)
  • On every pipeline, CI reruns the script with Python 3.12 and fails if any output differs from the files on this page. (evidence)

Not verified

  • Any real programme or delivery. The recording is NASA’s; nothing here says that a programme, a meter or a delivery meets EBU R 128, a broadcaster’s specification or a platform’s target.
  • Tech 3341 cases 7 and 8 (authentic programmes from the EBU test set), 11 and 14 (live meters) and 20 to 23 (true peak of signals synthesized at four times the rate), and the loudness range of EBU Tech 3342.
  • Sample rates other than 48 kHz, channel layouts beyond 5.0, and the object-based and advanced sound systems of BS.1770-5 Annex 3.
  • Limiting or any processing beyond a gain: the pathway plans a normalisation and never changes the recording’s dynamics.
  • Use by real learners: no learner has run these katas yet.

Professional review: None. No broadcast audio engineer, delivery quality-control specialist or mastering engineer has reviewed this pathway; a real delivery needs the checks its broadcaster or platform asks for.

Terms of the field

LUFS (LKFS)
Loudness units relative to full scale: a programme’s K-weighted, gated loudness on a scale where 1 LU is 1 dB; BS.1770 writes LKFS and EBU R 128 LUFS for the same unit.
K-weighting
The two filters BS.1770 applies before measuring power: a shelf that models the head and a high-pass that cuts the lowest frequencies (Tables 1 and 2).
Gating
Leaving quiet 400 ms blocks out of the integrated loudness: those below -70 LKFS, then those more than 10 LU below the loudness of the rest (BS.1770-5, equations 5 to 7).
Integrated loudness
The gated loudness of a whole programme, the one number R 128 normalises to -23.0 LUFS.
Momentary and short-term loudness
Ungated loudness in a sliding window of 0.4 s and of 3 s, what a meter shows while the programme plays (EBU Tech 3341).
True peak
The largest value of the waveform between samples, estimated by over-sampling, in dBTP; it can be higher than the largest sample (BS.1770-5 Annex 2).
Normalisation
Changing a programme’s level by one gain so that its integrated loudness reaches a target, keeping its true peak under the permitted maximum (EBU R 128).

Procedures practised

  1. Inspect a recording’s format, sample peaks, levels and channels before metering it (MED-001)
  2. Measure programme loudness and true peak with a meter checked against published test signals (MED-002)
  3. Plan a loudness normalisation to EBU R 128 and check it against the true-peak limit (MED-003)
