Start free

Teamwork kata · about 10 minutes

Turn a meeting into momentum

Make the next action obvious, keep the evidence, and catch what nobody actually agreed.

Start with the notes

The finished outcome

A small board your team can act on

Rin · 13 Oct

Check keyboard flow; save the findings. Folder location: unknown.

Sam · 14 Oct

Ask the coordinator to review the draft. Review requested does not mean approved.

Open question

Who owns the volunteer demo, and when? The meeting did not decide.

Ingredients: the fictional notes and prompt below; any assistant and model in a phone app, browser or trusted CLI; a place to keep your reviewed draft; and a human decision-maker. No plugin, skill, MCP server, account connection or paid service is required by this exercise. Provider access and costs vary.

Start now

Turn notes into commitments

  1. Copy the complete prompt into your chosen assistant. You can also make the board yourself.
  2. Compare every owner, date and status with the notes. Mark anything absent as unknown.
  3. Save the board where your team has agreed; ask the relevant people to confirm before changing a shared tracker or sending a message.
Or select and copy the text.
Reveal the independent check

There are two explicit commitments: Rin's keyboard check by 13 October and Sam's request for review by 14 October. Lee shared a draft; no date or owner was assigned to a volunteer demo. The shared folder location is unknown. The notes say the draft is not reviewed and a public launch is not approved. The next sync is 12 October at 15:00 UTC.

Write an update people can use

For the next sync, report the goal, what is actually complete, each commitment and its evidence, the open owner and folder questions, and the next time to check in. Ask for help early when a blocker needs another person's decision. If a date becomes unrealistic, propose a revised date and obtain agreement rather than silently changing it.

Make it a team habit

Move from activity to an outcome

  1. Agree: confirm the goal, decision-maker, responsibilities, availability and what done looks like.
  2. Work together: use the check-in to inspect progress, ask for help, pair on a difficult task and update the plan. Not every useful contribution happens in a meeting.
  3. Leave evidence: save an authorized link or artifact, say what changed and what still needs review, and agree on the next action.
  4. Improve: review the outcome and invite corrections. Missing records or automated activity counts alone do not prove that work was not done; ask for context and supporting evidence. People make personnel decisions through their own appropriate processes.
