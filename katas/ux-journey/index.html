02 / Work backward

Map what actually happens

The diagram below is a Dojo-created, editable SVG wireframe of three states of the same screen. It describes the working example rather than claiming a separate design-tool file already exists.

Download the editable SVG wireframe

  1. Start: see Basil, Mint and Sunflower.
  2. Choose: select Loves sun to show Basil and Sunflower, or Likes shade to show Mint. Select All plants to return to the complete list.
  3. Act and undo: mark a plant as planted and tap again to undo. Reloading resets all choices.

The example uses fixed data: it does not demonstrate accounts, a server, an empty result, a payment flow or persistent storage. Add and test those separately if your own concept needs them.

04 / Your turn

Give your agent a UX brief

Open any AI assistant on a phone or computer, or use a CLI harness you trust. No special plugin, paid design tool or connected account is needed for this prompt. Check your chosen service’s terms and costs.

Or select and copy the text.

Ingredients: your goal and audience; the prompt; your chosen assistant and model; and a place to sketch. Optional: use a design tool for your own editable screens. Do not share sensitive customer information with an assistant without permission.

Choose a phone, browser or desktop setup

For the first pass, use an assistant app or browser and sketch on paper. If you already use a CLI, paste the same brief into your trusted harness and review any file or command permissions. See phone, Windows, macOS and Linux setup options.

05 / Build and verify

Turn a sketch into a prototype

Choose a learning resource, create your own boards or frames, and connect a starting screen to a detail screen and back. Test it on your device. These are external provider tutorials; account, access and plan limits may apply.

  • Penpot: connect boards and prototype interactions
  • Figma: create a low-fidelity clickable photo gallery
  • Figma: beginner design course and videos

Optional integrations have their own compatibility, permissions and installation requirements. This kata does not install or connect an MCP server, plugin or skill, and does not claim that an agent can edit your design file.

Check your work

  1. Try each route, including the way back and any empty or error state your design includes.
  2. Test on a narrow screen and with a keyboard if available. Check labels, visible focus and readable contrast; check your text colours.
  3. Compare the prototype to your journey and design choices. Record what you tested, what is untested and what changed.
Quick check: does a screenshot prove a prototype is accessible?

No. A screenshot cannot demonstrate keyboard operation, focus order, labels or screen-reader behavior. Test the actual interactions and seek feedback from people using assistive technology.

Randori: change the goal

Adapt the same approach to a reading list for a busy commuter. Design the empty state and a clear way to undo an action, then ask someone to attempt your journey. Record what you learned; do not invent results from a usability test.
