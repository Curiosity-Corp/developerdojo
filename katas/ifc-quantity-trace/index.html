3D · BIM · quantity evidence

Can you trace the quantity?

Follow one wall and one opening from a real IFC sample to a checkable practice estimate.

Start the kataOpen the IFC sample

See the outcome first

One face: 6 − 1 = 5 m²

Under the stated practice assumptions, a 3 m × 2 m wall face is 6 m². Subtract one 1 m × 1 m opening to get 5 m². This is a hand-worked estimate from the example's geometry, not a stored IFC quantity or an automated validation result.

This simplified elevation illustrates the calculation. It is not a dimensioned construction drawing, a measured building or proof that a product can be installed.

Study the evidence

Follow the references

Open the pinned buildingSMART IFC4 sample and use find-in-page for the entity numbers. The source is plain text. Entity numbers beginning with # identify lines in this file, not stable identifiers across exports.

  1. Units: #8 sets project length to millimetres; #9 sets area to square metres. Divide each length in millimetres by 1,000 before multiplying in metres.
  2. Wall: wall #45 uses profile #72, bounded by polyline #73. Points #74–#77 span 3,000 mm by 300 mm; #71 extrudes it 2,000 mm in the #27 direction. For one broad face, use the 3,000 mm length and 2,000 mm height.
  3. Opening: opening #80 uses profile #88, bounded by polyline #89, spanning 1,000 mm by 300 mm, and is extruded 1,000 mm by #87. Local placement #81 uses point #83; #85 relates it to the wall.
  4. Boundary: the wall's FireRating at #52 is an empty label. An empty field is not a verified rating. The sample does not contain an IFCELEMENTQUANTITY set; this exercise derives an estimate rather than reading an existing quantity set.

Assemble your stack

Ask, then verify

Use any assistant available in a phone app or browser, or a trusted CLI harness on a computer. You need the prompt, the extracted observations and yourself as reviewer; no plugin, skill, MCP server or connector is required. Provider access and costs vary. Do not share confidential model data without permission.

Or select and copy the text.
Check the arithmetic
  1. Wall: 3,000 mm ÷ 1,000 = 3 m; 2,000 mm ÷ 1,000 = 2 m. Gross one-face area: 3 × 2 = 6 m².
  2. Opening: 1,000 mm = 1 m in width and height. Deduction: 1 × 1 = 1 m².
  3. Practice result: 6 − 1 = 5 m². This excludes the opposite face, edges and reveals. The thickness is not another face-area multiplier.
  4. Confirm that the assistant cites the source, keeps the empty fire rating unresolved and states its assumptions instead of asserting an approval.

For another method, study the official IfcOpenShell geometry documentation, which distinguishes actual side area from projected area. This kata has not run that software or compared its output with the hand calculation.

Adapt · Randori

Change the rule

A reviewer asks for both broad faces under the same simple assumptions. Calculate the area, and state which extra surfaces remain excluded. Do not change the source IFC or imply this is a contractual measurement rule.

Reveal one check

Two identical broad faces give 2 × 5 = 10 m². Edges, opening reveals, coatings, waste, site conditions and project-specific measurement rules remain outside this simple estimate.

For real work, a qualified professional must check the source model, measurement rules, openings, geometry, units, quantities and any required approval. This exercise does not establish safety, code compliance, a price, construction readiness or a professional certification.
