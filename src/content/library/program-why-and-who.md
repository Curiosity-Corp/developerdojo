You may already know how to build parts of a product: a front end, some full stack, a chat with an AI agent. Those skills let you build **parts**. This program teaches the skill many builders do not have yet: seeing how every piece of a product ties together, and owning the plan for the whole product.

## Why it matters

A single repository is easy to hold in your head. A real product is not one repository. It has a public website, an app, small cloud services, a payment gateway, a customer database, and the people who use all of it. An AI agent works on one task in one place at a time. It does not know what the customer said on the phone last week, what the sales team promised, or what money is left. A person who can hold the whole picture is rare and valuable.

That person is the **Master Developer**.

## What a Master Developer is

A Master Developer orchestrates across many repositories. They steer AI agents to build products that work across several disciplines and make customers happy. Two sentences carry the whole job:

- **The human owns the idea and the epic roadmap.**
- **The agents build the repositories.**

An agent can turn a clear milestone into issues and code very well. An agent cannot hold a whole product in its head. That is your job.

## Who it is for

The program is for people who can already make parts of a product and want to see how everything ties together.

| You are | Good fit? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A front-end or full-stack builder who works with AI agents | Yes | You can build parts; you now learn to plan the whole |
| A product or project person early in your career | Yes | You know the words; you now use them on a real product |
| Someone who has finished the free foundation track | Yes | This is the next step |
| Someone who wants a certificate without doing the work | No | Your roadmap and your capstone are the proof, not a certificate |
| Someone who wants an agent to write the plan for them | No | The one rule of the program forbids it |

You do not need to finish the foundation track first. You can start while you finish it. If you already did a course on the same topic, such as agile or GitLab project management, skip the duplicate.

## How it fits with the foundation track

The free foundation track teaches the tools and the words. This program asks you to use them on a whole product, with real repositories, real customers and real agents.

| | Foundation track | This program |
|---|---|---|
| The question it answers | Do I know the tools and the words? | Can I own a whole product's plan? |
| Shape | A set of short courses over about 12 weeks, in five areas: product sense, iteration, launch and experience, orchestration, tooling | Hands-on weeks on one real product |
| Evidence | Provider certificates and screenshots of finished courses | Workbooks, epics in git, a defended roadmap |
| Planning | Learning items on a board | Your product's epics, milestones and roadmap |
| Coach | Walk the board with a coach | A weekly checkpoint and a final review |

## Key ideas

- **Parts versus whole.** Building a part is a skill. Owning the whole is a different skill.
- **Humans decide, agents build.** This is the rule that organises the rest.
- **Evidence over certificates.** A certificate shows you know the words. A defended roadmap shows you can do the job.
- **Real product, real customers.** You work on one product, not a toy exercise. A fictional product (TrailMug) is used in the pages to show the shape.

## Working with AI agents

- **May:** explain a word you do not know, summarise a long document so you can read the original faster, check spelling after you write.
- **May not:** choose your product, write your idea paragraph, or tell you what the whole product is. You must look at it yourself.
- **A safe brief:** "Explain in plain words what an epic is in agile planning. Give two examples. Do not write one for my product."

## Common mistakes

1. **Treating this as a coding course.** The planning is the course.
2. **Skipping the "whole product" idea** and describing one repository.
3. **Believing a certificate is the goal.**
4. **Picking a product you cannot talk to customers about.**
5. **Letting an agent write the idea paragraph.**

## The 2030 Product Development Manager lens

- **You decide:** which product, what it is for, and what the whole looks like.
- **You delegate to agents:** reading documents, listing repositories, drafting issues from milestones you wrote.
- **A sample brief:** "List every repository in this group with its description. Do not guess how they connect."
- **You verify by:** opening two of them and checking the description against the code.
- **Disciplines involved:** product, engineering, design, marketing, sales, finance and operations.
- **On the agent ladder:** this program moves you from **use** towards **delegate**, **orchestrate** and **govern**.
