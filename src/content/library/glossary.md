Short meanings, in alphabetical order. Each page of the library also explains its words the first time they appear.

## A

**Acceptance criteria.** Checks that prove an issue or epic is done. A person must be able to test each one.

**ADR (architecture decision record).** A short file that records a decision, the options, the reason and how to leave. Also called a decision record.

**Agile.** A way of building a product in small steps. You release a little, learn from real use, then change the plan. See [Choose your rhythm](/library/tracks/agile-delivery/01-choose-your-rhythm).

**Agile Manifesto.** A short statement from 2001. It values people, working products, working with customers and responding to change.

**Agile mindset.** A way of thinking: treat mistakes as lessons, welcome change and work closely with others.

**Agile team.** A small group, often 5 to 11 people, with all the skills to deliver. The team decides how to do its own work.

**Agile transformation.** Slowly changing a whole organisation so its habits, culture and processes follow agile ideas.

**AGPL.** A licence with network copyleft. If you modify the program and let people use it over a network, you must offer them your source.

**AI agent.** An AI coding assistant that reads your project and builds tasks. It does the task in front of it. It does not own the plan.

## B

**B2B2C.** Business to business to consumer. A business pays. Its consumers use the product.

**Backlog.** An ordered list of work waiting to be done: wanted features and fixes. The top items come next. See [The backlog](/library/tracks/agile-delivery/02-backlog-and-stories).

**Bottleneck.** The step in a process where work piles up and slows everything after it.

**Branch.** A separate line of changes in git.

**Brand.** What people think of when they hear your product name.

**Burndown chart.** A chart of the work still left against time. In a healthy sprint the line falls to zero by the end.

**Burnup chart.** A chart of work done against all the work planned. It also shows when the plan grows.

**Bus factor.** The number of people who must leave before a project stalls.

## C

**CAB (change advisory board).** A group that judges risky changes. In git it can be code owners plus approval rules.

**Cadence.** A steady, repeating rhythm, such as a new sprint every two weeks. People can plan and give feedback on the same days.

**Catalogue (product catalogue).** A structured description of a product in four layers: Master, ORBITAL, ANDROID and Tech.

**Catalogue entry.** A small file for one product or service with owner, tier, repos, dependencies and service levels.

**Ceremonies.** The regular Scrum meetings: sprint planning, daily scrum, sprint review and sprint retrospective. They give the team a steady structure.

**Change record.** The record of a change: reason, risk, review, checks. In git, the merge request.

**CI/CD.** Continuous integration and delivery. Automatic steps that test, build and deliver changes.

**Commit.** One saved step in git, with a message.

**Configuration item.** Anything that must be managed to deliver a service: a repo, an app, a database.

**Copyleft.** A licence idea: if you pass on the code, you must pass on the same freedoms.

**CRM.** Customer relationship management. A system that records leads and customers.

**Customer.** The person who pays. Often not the user.

## D

**Daily Scrum (stand-up).** A short daily meeting where the team shares progress and agrees a plan for the next day.

**Decision log.** A dated list of decisions with the question, answer, reason and name.

**Demo.** A short walk-through that shows the product working.

**Desired state.** What git says should exist.

**DevOps.** Developers and operations people working as one team, with automatic delivery (CI/CD), so changes reach users quickly and safely.

**Drift.** Any difference between desired state and actual state.

## E

**Epic.** A whole-product outcome across several repos. Weeks to months. Written by a human.

**Epic manager.** The human who owns the epics and the roadmap of a product.

**Error budget.** The amount of failure a service-level target allows.

**Estimation.** Guessing how big a piece of work is, in points or hours, before you start. It is a guess, not a promise. See [Plan the cycle](/library/tracks/agile-delivery/03-plan-the-cycle-and-define-done).

**Extreme Programming (XP).** An agile method with strict engineering habits: write tests first, work in pairs and release often.

## F

**Fit %.** How much of your needs a tool already meets. MUST needs count double.

**Fix forward.** Fix problems with a new change. Do not revert shipped work.

**Fork.** A full copy of a project that you maintain.

## G

**Git.** A tool that records every change to files.

**GitOps.** A controller makes a system match what is in git, continuously.

**Golden path.** The easy, safe, standard way to go from idea to production.

## I

**IaC (infrastructure as code).** Cloud setup written as files in git.

**Idempotent.** Running it twice gives the same result as running it once.

**Incident.** An unplanned break or drop in quality of a service.

**Integration.** A tool from another company that your product uses.

**Issue.** One task. One agent can finish it in one merge request.

**Iteration.** A short, fixed block of time, usually 1 to 4 weeks, in which a team finishes a chosen set of work.

**ITIL.** A set of practices for managing IT services.

## J

**Journey (user or customer).** The steps a person takes to reach a goal, with thoughts and feelings.

## K

**Kanban.** A method that shows work on a board and limits how much is in progress at once, so work keeps flowing to done.

**KPI (key performance indicator).** A number that shows progress toward a goal.

## L

**Label.** A tag on an issue, such as `status-blocked`.

**Lean.** A way of working that aims to give customers more value with less waste.

**Licence.** The legal paper that says what you may do with code.

## M

**Merge request (MR).** A proposal to add a change to the main branch. Also called a pull request.

**Merge train.** A queue that tests each merge request on top of the others before it merges.

**Milestone.** A time-boxed slice of work with a goal and a date.

**Minimum viable product (MVP).** The smallest version of a product that people can really use. You release it to learn what customers need.

**Mockup.** A drawing of a screen before it is built.

## O

**Open source.** Software whose code you may read, change and run under a public licence.

**ORBITAL.** The catalogue layer for objective, resources, budget, indicators, transport, activities and logistics.

**Orphan issue.** An issue with no epic.

## P

**Patch.** A small file describing one change to code.

**Patch set.** A short list of patches applied on top of a pinned upstream version.

**Persona.** A short profile of a typical person, built from real interviews.

**Pipeline.** The automatic steps that test and deliver a change.

**Product Owner.** The person who decides what the team builds next and keeps the backlog in order. They answer for the product's value.

**Product Package.** The final assessment of the Developer path. Eight parts.

## Q

**Quality triangle.** A model with three corners: time, cost and quality. Change one corner and the others are affected.

## R

**Refinement (grooming).** Regularly reading, clarifying and sizing backlog items, so they are ready before a sprint starts.

**Repository (repo).** A folder of files with a full change history.

**Roadmap.** Epics laid out in order over time.

**Runbook.** Step-by-step instructions for running or fixing a service.

## S

**SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework).** A set of guidance for using lean and agile methods in a large company with many teams.

**SBOM (software bill of materials).** A list of the parts inside software and their licences.

**Scrum.** An agile framework with fixed-length sprints, set roles and set meetings. The team builds the product in small steps. See [Choose your rhythm](/library/tracks/agile-delivery/01-choose-your-rhythm).

**Scrum Master.** The person who helps the team use Scrum well and clears obstacles out of its way.

**Scrum of Scrums.** A meeting where one person from each Scrum team shares news, so the teams stay in step and see what depends on what.

**Secret.** A password, key or token. Never kept in git. Only its name is.

**Service level (SLI, SLO, SLA).** An indicator you measure, an objective you aim for, and an agreement you promise.

**SPDX id.** A standard short name for a licence, such as `MIT` or `Apache-2.0`.

**Sprint.** The Scrum word for an iteration: a fixed block of time, with a goal, in which the team finishes chosen work.

**Sprint planning.** The meeting at the start of a sprint where the team chooses what to deliver and how to do it.

**Sprint retrospective.** A team-only meeting after the sprint review. It finds what to improve in the next sprint. See [Review, retrospect and improve](/library/tracks/agile-delivery/04-review-retrospect-and-improve).

**Sprint review.** A meeting at the end of a sprint where the team shows what is done and updates the backlog.

**Stakeholder.** A person or group affected by the product or able to affect it.

**Story points.** Numbers a team uses to compare the size of pieces of work by effort, complexity and risk. They are not hours.

## T

**Tech debt.** A promise of future work caused by code you must maintain.

**Touch point.** Any place where a person meets your product.

## U

**Upstream.** The original project that you adopt or fork.

**User.** The person who uses the product.

## V

**Velocity.** How many story points a team finishes in one sprint. Teams use it to forecast their next sprints.

## W

**Waste.** Any activity that uses time or money without giving the customer value. Lean works to remove it.

**Waterfall.** A traditional method where work goes in a fixed order: plan, build, test, release. Often used as the opposite of agile.

**Webhook.** A message one system sends to another when something happens.

## Y

**YAML.** A plain-text format for settings, written as indented lines.
