## In short

The Developer path is the first rank of the dojo. In six weeks you take one product from idea to a package. Week 1, you draw the whole product on one page. Week 2, you meet the people who buy and use it. Week 3, you turn their problems into epics. Week 4, you defend your roadmap to others. Week 5, you give the product a brand and a website. Week 6, you show a catalogue, a demo and your Product Package.

You write the epics yourself. AI agents may break your milestones into issues and build them. You say how you used AI. If you fall short, you get a coaching plan, not a failure. On the agent ladder, this path teaches use and delegate.

### Key words

- **Persona:** A short profile of a typical person, built from real interviews.
- **Journey (user or customer):** The steps a person takes to reach a goal, with thoughts and feelings.
- **Epic:** A whole-product outcome across several repos. Weeks to months. Written by a human.
- **Roadmap:** Epics laid out in order over time.
- **Product Package:** The final assessment of the Developer path. Eight parts.
- **Agent ladder:** Four rungs of skill with AI agents: use, delegate, orchestrate and govern. Each rung needs different skills.

The Developer path is the first rank of the dojo. In six weeks you take **one product** from idea to a package you can present. You do not just write code. You talk to customers, plan the whole product, and steer AI agents that do the building.

## What you need

- A product idea you care about. It can be small. You may use a real idea, or the fictional TrailMug used in these pages.
- About 8 to 10 hours a week.
- A free account on a git host such as gitlab.com or github.com.
- A coach or mentor if you can find one, or a study partner.

## The six weeks

| Week | Theme | You finish with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [See the whole product](/library/week-1-see-the-whole-product) | A one-page map of the product and customer interviews started |
| 2 | [Know the people](/library/week-2-know-the-people) | Personas, touch points and journeys |
| 3 | [From problems to epics](/library/week-3-problems-to-epics) | A roadmap of 4 to 8 epics, written by you |
| 4 | [Defend the roadmap](/library/week-4-defend-the-roadmap) | A reviewed roadmap, stakeholder map and agent-ready epics |
| 5 | [Brand, website and mockups](/library/week-5-brand-website-mockups) | A brand name, a simple website and next-version mockups |
| 6 | [Catalogue, demo and final package](/library/week-6-catalogue-demo-package) | A catalogue, a demo and your Product Package presentation |

## Weekly goals and outputs

Every week has one goal and one output you can show your Sensei. The full table, with the checkpoint for each week, is in [the two levels, the weeks and the capstone](/library/program-two-levels-and-capstone).

| Week | Goal | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Draw the whole product on one page | Repository map, integrations register, one-page picture |
| 2 | Know the people who find, buy and use it | Personas, touch points, user and customer journeys |
| 3 | Turn gaps into epics | Four to eight epics in git, ordered with reasons |
| 4 | Defend the roadmap | Reviewed roadmap, dated milestones, a ten-minute presentation |
| 5 | Give the product a face | Brand sheet, published page, mockups linked to epics |
| 6 | Show it is real | Catalogue, demo and Product Package |

## The final assessment

The last week ends with a **Product Package**. It is a bundle of evidence that you own the product. See [Product Package standard](/library/product-package-standard) and [Product Package rubric](/library/product-package-rubric).

## How to study each week

1. Read the week page.
2. Do the exercises in your own project, in git.
3. Use the templates in the Templates section.
4. Pick two or three items from the [Learning path](/library/learning-path-how-to-use).
5. On Friday, fill in the self-check and write a [weekly note](/library/template-weekly-note).

## The rules still apply

All five rules of [The Way](/library/the-way-five-rules) apply from day one. The most important for you: **you write your own epics**, and **you say how you used AI**.

## Passing

You pass when you reach level 3 ("Competent") on all rubric skills and no skill is below level 2. If you do not, you get a coaching plan, not a failure. See [how you are assessed](/library/program-assessment-and-pass-level).

## After the Developer path

Next ranks: Master Developer (platform, production launch, AI-agent orchestration) and Sensei (coaching, hosting live builds, reviewing work).

## Working with AI agents

- **May:** explain a word, check your spelling, break a milestone you wrote into issues and build them, summarise a long page so you can read the original faster.
- **May not:** write, reorder or improve your epics, invent personas or quotes, or fill your workbooks. See [AI use and disclosure](/library/program-ai-use-and-disclosure).

## The 2030 Product Development Manager lens

- **You decide:** the product, the epics, their order and what you will not do.
- **You delegate to agents:** issues for milestones you approved, lists, link checks and summaries.
- **A sample brief:** "Break this milestone into issues of at most two days, each with a done-when line. List your questions. Do not change scope."
- **You verify by:** reading each issue against the milestone and removing any that adds scope.
- **Disciplines involved:** product, design, engineering, marketing, sales, finance and operations.
- **On the agent ladder:** the Developer path teaches **use** and **delegate**. See [the 2030 Product Development Manager](/library/the-2030-product-development-manager).
