**Fictional example.** Shiftwren is made up. Its company, people, interviews, quotes, numbers, dates and prices are invented for teaching. No real organisation, person or customer is described. Where a number appears, it is round and marked "example". Your own product needs your own evidence.

## Why a second example

[TrailMug](/library/templates-overview) is a physical product: reusable cups. The [real-product case study](/library/case-study-danhup-the-product-on-one-page) shows the method on a product with real findings. Many learners build something else: a software service, a tool that other businesses use, or a community project. Shiftwren is a worked example for them. Its customer is not its user, it holds personal data, and it has no physical parts.

## The product

**Shiftwren** helps volunteer-run food pantries fill their weekly shifts. A coordinator posts the shifts. Volunteers confirm with one tap on a phone. When someone cancels, Shiftwren asks the next person on the list. It is a pilot: one prototype app, one web page and a few tools.

| Person (all made up) | Role in the story |
|---|---|
| Noor | The learner and product manager. She writes every epic |
| Ines | Pantry coordinator. Chooses the tool and uses it daily |
| Ravi and Mei | Volunteers. Ravi dislikes passwords. Mei reads English as a second language |
| Jonas | A pantry treasurer. Approves the cost. Known from one interview, so a guess |
| Priya | The volunteer builder who owns the app |

Each Shiftwren page fills every field of its blank template, so you can see what a finished one looks like. The TrailMug examples are on the template pages and in the week pages.

## How to use the example

1. Read the Developer week page first.
2. Do your own work from your own evidence: your interviews, your repositories, your numbers.
3. Open the matching Shiftwren page and compare shape, not content.
4. Check yourself: could you fill your blank template, field by field, the way the example does? Where the example has a field and you have none, you have found a gap.
5. Write your epics and roadmap yourself. See [people write epics](/library/people-write-epics).

## What the example cannot do

- It does not know your customers, your tools or your law.
- Its interviews are invented. Yours must be real, and every quote needs a source.
- Its name check is part of the story. It is not a trademark search or legal advice.
- It is not a recipe for a food pantry, a price list or a market claim.

## Working with AI agents

An AI agent drafted these seven pages from the Week 1 to 6 pages, the templates and the style guide. A person at Developer Dojo reviewed them before they were published. In the story, Noor writes the epics and the roadmap, and an agent does only routine work: summarising notes, breaking an approved milestone into issues, checking contrast and cross-checking names. Each such use is disclosed on its page.

You **may** use an agent for the same routine work, with a written brief. You **may not** use one to write your epics, roadmap, personas or quotes. Say how you used AI. See [disclose AI use](/library/disclose-ai-use).

## The 2030 Product Development Manager lens

- **You decide:** what your product is, who it serves and which parts of this example are useful to you.
- **You delegate:** checks against the example, for instance "List every field of the repository map template that my file leaves empty. Do not fill them."
- **You verify by:** reading the empty fields yourself and finding the evidence for each.
- **Disciplines involved:** product, design, engineering, privacy, finance, operations and sales.
- **On the agent ladder:** the example teaches **use** and **delegate**. Epics stay human only.
