A product has a face. People meet it in a name, a few sentences, a colour and a page long before they meet a feature. If the face is confusing, the product is confusing, however good the code behind it.

This track covers the front of the product: the brand, the website and the mockups of what comes next.

## Who this track is for

You have a product idea, a roadmap and some evidence about the people it serves. You now need a name, a front door and pictures of what you plan to build. You do not need to be a designer. You need to be careful, honest and willing to watch a person use what you made.

## What the track covers

- **Brand:** what people think of when they hear your name. We keep it concrete: a name, a promise, a tone of voice, two colours and a font.
- **Website:** the front door. A small site in git, in plain words, that loads on a phone and can be read by everyone.
- **Mockups:** drawings of screens before they are built. Each one is linked to an epic, because a mockup with no epic is decoration.

## What changes when agents do the building

An agent can build a website from a brand sheet in an hour. That is the reason to be exact about the sheet. It can also generate a hundred logo ideas, a dozen colour schemes and a thousand words of marketing copy. The risk is not too little output. It is too much that sounds good and means nothing.

- **Promises are yours.** An agent will happily write "the fastest, most trusted solution". You decide what you can honestly promise, and you check every claim on the page.
- **Taste and judgement are yours.** An agent has no eyes on your user. Watching a real person use the page is a job no agent can do for you.
- **Accessibility is checked, not assumed.** Agents often produce pages that look fine and fail for people using screen readers or keyboards. You write checks and you run them.
- **Names carry legal and market risk.** An agent cannot tell you whether a name is free to use in your country. Search yourself, and ask an adviser before launch.

## The five lessons

1. **Name and promise.** Choose a name with evidence, and write one sentence you can keep.
2. **Voice, colour and type, and the brand sheet.** Plain words, two colours, one font and a single page that agents can follow.
3. **Mockups that link to epics.** Wireframes and mockups, and how to draw the three screens that matter.
4. **Build and publish the website.** A small site from git, with a free static host, brief for an agent and checks you run.
5. **Test accessibility and usability.** Check the basics, watch one person and fix what you see.

Each lesson follows the same shape as the rest of the Dojo: why it matters, key ideas, steps, a worked example with the fictional product **TrailMug**, working with AI agents, common mistakes, a practice exercise ending in a file in your repo, a checklist and links to free outside learning.

## The 2030 Product Development Manager at work

The manager is part strategist, part editor and part quality gate. They decide what the brand promises, what the site says and which screens are worth drawing. They delegate drafts, layouts, code and first checks to agents with a written brief and a brand sheet as the standard. They verify by reading every word, running the accessibility checks and watching a person use the site.

Many disciplines meet here. **Marketing and sales** want a clear message. **Design** owns how it looks and feels. **Legal and privacy** care about names, claims and notices. **Engineering** cares about performance and maintainability. **Support** cares about what customers will ask. **Operations** cares about hosting and keeping it running. **Finance** cares about domain and tool costs.

Each lesson places its work on the agent ladder: **use** one agent, **delegate** whole tasks with a brief, **orchestrate** several sessions, and **govern** them with written policy, hooks and gates. The agent orchestration track (`/library/tracks/agent-orchestration/`) covers the upper rungs. For brand work, the brand sheet is policy, and a pipeline check for contrast, links and page weight is a gate.
