Ranks · belts · independent review

What each rank assesses

A rank and a belt answer different questions. This map shows what each one looks for, the proposed rubrics and how an independent review would work.

See the ranksSee the review pilot

Proposed, not approved: Every rubric and belt stage on this page is a proposal for the program policy review. The existing rank certificate requirements stay exactly as they are. No belt is issued, and no pilot review has run yet.

Three ranks, three questions

Developer, Master Developer and Sensei are the same in every field. Each rank asks its own question. None of them is earned by collecting belts.

Developer

Can this person develop one outcome from start to finish and show evidence that it works?

Assesses

  • Owning one outcome: the audience, the result and the checks that show success.
  • Directing AI agents and tools, with the stack and permissions recorded so another person can repeat the run.
  • Checking the result honestly, including the failures and the parts that were not tested.

Does not assess

  • Which field the work is in. Ability in a field is what a belt shows.
  • Speed, volume of output or the number of tools used.

Today

The Developer certificate does not change: a Sensei reviews a Product Package against the six-skill rubric, and a separate Maintainer decides. Product Package rubric

Unseen task in a review

One changed requirement in your own field, chosen by the reviewer and revealed when the session starts. Ask for a Developer review

Master Developer

Can this person carry their judgment to a tool family or field they did not practice in?

Assesses

  • Transfer: solving an unseen brief in a different tool family or field, with checks the reviewer chose.
  • Recovery: diagnosing a changed tool or an injected failure without a recipe, and repeating the same checks.
  • Judgment: defending trade-offs and naming what remains unverified, in a live review without notes.

Does not assess

  • Repeating a kata the learner already practiced, however well it is done.
  • Depth in one field alone. A belt shows field depth.

Today

The Master Developer certificate does not change: four gates, a final exam and an oral review observed by a Sensei or mentor, and a separate Maintainer decides. Master Developer assessment

Unseen task in a review

A brief in a tool family or field you did not practice, and a tool withdrawn or a failure injected while you work on it. Ask for a Master Developer review

Sensei

Can this person teach and review a technique that works in many fields, and show how it adapts in each?

Assesses

  • Teaching: another learner reproduces a generalizable technique and checks it on their own.
  • Adaptation: naming what changes when the technique moves to another field, including the checks and qualified review that field adds.
  • Reviewing: judging another person's evidence independently, fairly and privately.

Does not assess

  • Seniority, popularity or contribution points on their own.
  • Professional approval in any regulated field.

Today

Sensei stays by application or invitation. The Sensei handbook sets how a Sensei reviews, and the certificate process sets who decides. Sensei role and limits

Unseen task in a review

Teaching a technique to a second volunteer under a constraint the reviewer chooses, adapting it to a second field named on the day, and reviewing a packet with one known defect. Ask for a Sensei review

Belts answer a different question

Can this person show ability in one field, using the checks and standards of that field?

  • A field artifact checked against the standards of that field, not only against the learner's own checks.
  • Adapting inside the field when a Randori exercise changes a requirement, a tool or a source.
  • Knowing where the field needs independent or qualified professional review.

A belt is about one field. A rank is about developing in any field, transferring judgment and teaching others. A belt stage never confers a rank on its own.

Proposed belt progression

  1. White: See and explain Proposed
    Identify the outcome, its audience and a check for success. Explain what the harness, model and instructions each do.
  2. Yellow: Follow a kata Proposed
    Reproduce a documented result, record your stack and show that you checked it against the recipe.
  3. Green: Adapt the technique Proposed
    Change a requirement or tool, explain your decision and compare the result against the same checks.
  4. Brown: Solve an unfamiliar challenge Proposed
    Choose your own stack, respond to a failure or new constraint, and defend the evidence in a Randori exercise.
  5. Black: Improve and teach Proposed
    Combine or invent a repeatable technique, explain its limits, and help another learner reproduce and evaluate it.

Practice the belt stages in your field

Specialist belts

Can this person do the work of one field, checked the way that field checks it, when the situation changes?

A specialist belt is a belt stage in one field, reviewed against that field's own checks. It is reviewed independently at the Brown and Black stages. Brown is the first stage that asks the learner to defend their evidence under an unseen change, so it is the first one an independent reviewer looks at. White, Yellow and Green are practiced with your own checklist on the belts page.

What every specialist belt asks Proposed

SPB-1 Your own field artifact, checked the field's way

Observed when
The learner brings an artifact of their own in the field, not the worked example, and the reviewer reruns the checks the field pathway names on it and gets the results the learner recorded.
Not yet observed when
The artifact is the worked example, only the learner ran the checks, or the reviewer cannot rerun them from the record.
Requires
Independent judgment
Evidence
The actual artifacts · Reproducible configuration · Tests and checks

SPB-2 The field Randori, unseen

Observed when
The reviewer chooses a change of the kind the field Randori describes, one the learner has not practiced, and reveals it after the work starts. The learner adapts, reruns the same field checks and records what changed.
Not yet observed when
The learner already practiced that exact change, drops a field check to get a result, or does not record what changed.
Requires
Unseen constraint · Changed tool or failure · Independent judgment
Evidence
Failures · Tests and checks · Review log

SPB-3 Field limits and qualified review

Observed when
The learner says what the field checks do not cover and where the field needs a qualified professional, and claims no approval that a Dojo review cannot give.
Not yet observed when
The result is presented as approved, compliant or ready for real use, or the field's own limits are missing.
Requires
Independent judgment · Domain adaptations
Evidence
Decision evidence · Review log

The unseen task: A change of the kind the field Randori describes, one you have not practiced, revealed after the work starts. It is not one of the published Randori cards as written.

A review record for a specialist belt also checks the stage itself, using the proof of the Brown or Black stage in the progression above.

Proposed rubrics

Advanced criteria always require an unseen constraint, a changed tool or a failure, and independent judgment. Master Developer also requires transfer to another tool family or field. Sensei also requires teaching or reviewing a technique that works across fields, with its domain adaptations.

A reviewer records each criterion as Observed, Not yet observed or Unclear, with an evidence reference. There is no score and no pass mark. Absence of evidence is not a pass.

Developer: Foundation rubric Proposed

DEV-1 Outcome and checks

Observed when
The learner states the audience, the intended result and the checks before building, and the artifact passes those checks when the reviewer runs them.
Not yet observed when
The checks are written after the result, are missing, or only the learner has run them.
Requires
Independent judgment
Evidence
The actual artifacts · Tests and checks

DEV-2 Reproducible stack

Observed when
The learner records the harness, model, instructions, tools, versions and permissions actually used, and the reviewer can repeat the main run from that record.
Not yet observed when
The stack is described in general terms, or the reviewer cannot repeat the run from the record.
Requires
Independent judgment
Evidence
Reproducible configuration · The actual artifacts

DEV-3 Failures on the record

Observed when
At least one real failure or wrong turn is recorded with what caused it, what changed and which check confirmed the repair.
Not yet observed when
The record shows only successes, or a failure is mentioned without the check that closed it.
Requires
Changed tool or failure
Evidence
Failures · Tests and checks · Review log

Master Developer: Advanced rubric Proposed

MD-1 Transfer to an unseen tool family or field

Observed when
Given a brief the learner has not seen, in a tool family or field other than the one practiced, the learner chooses and adapts a stack, and the result passes checks the reviewer chose.
Not yet observed when
The learner repeats a practiced recipe, stays in the practiced tool family, or only the learner checks the result.
Requires
Unseen constraint · Changed tool or failure · Independent judgment · Transfer to another tool family or field
Evidence
The actual artifacts · Reproducible configuration · Tests and checks · Decision evidence

MD-2 Recovery from a changed tool or failure

Observed when
When the reviewer withdraws a tool or injects a failure during the task, the learner diagnoses it, changes the approach, repeats the same checks and records what changed.
Not yet observed when
The learner restarts from scratch without a diagnosis, drops a check to get a pass, or hides the failure.
Requires
Unseen constraint · Changed tool or failure · Independent judgment
Evidence
Failures · Tests and checks · Review log

MD-3 Decisions defended without notes

Observed when
In the review the learner explains each main decision, the alternative they rejected, which check proves what, and what is still unverified, and adapts the plan when the reviewer adds a constraint.
Not yet observed when
Decisions are explained only as what an agent suggested, or the learner cannot say what remains unverified.
Requires
Unseen constraint · Changed tool or failure · Independent judgment · Transfer to another tool family or field
Evidence
Decision evidence · Review log

Sensei: Advanced rubric Proposed

SEN-1 Teach a generalizable technique

Observed when
Another learner reproduces the technique from the Sensei's explanation, then meets an unseen constraint the reviewer chose, and the Sensei coaches without doing the work for them.
Not yet observed when
The other learner can only follow the exact steps, or the Sensei takes over when the constraint changes.
Requires
Unseen constraint · Changed tool or failure · Independent judgment · Teaching or reviewing a technique
Evidence
The actual artifacts · Review log · Decision evidence

SEN-2 Domain adaptations

Observed when
The Sensei shows how the technique changes in a second field the reviewer names: the different inputs, tools, checks and the qualified review that field requires.
Not yet observed when
The technique is presented as identical in every field, or the field's own checks and approvals are missing.
Requires
Unseen constraint · Changed tool or failure · Independent judgment · Transfer to another tool family or field · Domain adaptations
Evidence
Decision evidence · Reproducible configuration · Tests and checks

SEN-3 Independent review of another person's work

Observed when
Given a packet they did not prepare, with a known defect added by the pilot coordinator, the Sensei records each criterion as observed, not yet observed or unclear, with evidence references, and finds the defect or says what stopped them.
Not yet observed when
The review repeats the learner's own claims, gives a score without evidence references, or shares material without consent.
Requires
Unseen constraint · Changed tool or failure · Independent judgment · Teaching or reviewing a technique
Evidence
Review log · Failures · Decision evidence

Independent review pilot

Status: Not run yet. The pilot tests whether these rubrics work with a real reviewer and real work. It uses the existing Sensei review guidance and certificate steps, and it adds no new way to earn a rank.

What the reviewer receives

  • The actual artifacts. The original work the learner made: files, a running prototype, a model, a report. Not screenshots or a summary of it.
  • Reproducible configuration. The harness, models, instructions, tools, versions and permissions used, enough for the reviewer to repeat the main run.
  • Tests and checks. The checks the learner ran and their real results, plus the checks the reviewer chose and runs independently.
  • Review log. What the reviewer opened, ran and observed, criterion by criterion, with evidence references and open questions.
  • Failures. What went wrong during the work and during the unseen task, what caused it, and which check confirmed each repair.
  • Decision evidence. The main decisions, the alternatives rejected and why, and what is still unverified at the end.

How a pilot review runs

  1. Agree scope, access and consent. The learner and reviewer agree in writing what the reviewer may open, keep and share. Learner work stays private by default and is never given to an AI service without permission. Uses: Sensei privacy and conduct
  2. Choose an independent reviewer. The reviewer has relevant expertise and no conflict of interest, and is a different person from whoever coordinates the pilot. Nobody reviews their own work. Uses: Certificate process: who decides
  3. Send the evidence packet. The learner prepares the six kinds of evidence above in their own repository or files and gives the reviewer read access. The Dojo website receives nothing. Uses: Review packet
  4. Set the unseen task. The reviewer, not the learner, chooses the unseen constraint, the changed tool or the injected failure, and reveals it only when the task starts. The learner works with it while the reviewer observes.
  5. Record each criterion. The reviewer records every criterion as observed, not yet observed or unclear, with an evidence reference, then gives one strength and one gap. There is no score and no pass mark. Uses: Run a 30 minute review
  6. Keep the decision separate. The pilot issues no belt and no rank. A rank certificate still follows the existing issue, correct and revoke steps, with a separate Maintainer deciding. Uses: Certificates
  7. Report what the pilot learned. The coordinator writes a short report: what worked, what was unclear, how long it took and which rubric changes to propose to the program policy review. Nothing identifies the learner without their consent.

During a pilot review, the reviewer can record each criterion with the review record. It keeps the record on the reviewer's device, will not finish without consent, independence and an evidence reference for every criterion, and fixes mistakes with dated corrections.

Boundaries

  • A checklist, attendance or time spent does not award a belt or a rank. Only reviewed evidence can inform a decision, and no belt is issued today.
  • Dojo assessment is not professional licensure or accreditation, and it is not permission to do regulated work.
  • The static site takes no authenticated submissions: it does not receive, store or send learner work, packets or review notes.
  • Regulated or safety-critical work still needs approval from a qualified professional, separate from any Dojo review.
  • Learner work and review notes stay private unless the learner gives written consent to share them.
  • The rubrics and belt names on this page are proposed. They change no existing rank requirement until the program policy review approves them.
