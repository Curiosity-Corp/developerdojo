The career path: Master Developer to Product Lead

Three roles in a row, what each one owns, how you show you are ready for the next, and what a certificate does and does not prove.

The program sits in a longer path. The free foundation track builds your base. The Master Developer program is the next step. After it, the path leads to people who run products.

Why it matters

People who see only the next course stop learning when the course ends. A clear path shows what comes after, and what proof is needed. It also stops you chasing certificates that do not move you forward.

The three roles

Role What you own How you show you are ready
Master Developer (this program) The epic roadmap for one product: the connection between customers, the app, the services and the plan A defended roadmap. Clear epics. Agents can build from your milestones with few questions.
Product Manager (next) The results of one product: users, revenue, priorities, release decisions, and the relationship with sales, support and finance Your epics deliver measured outcomes. You say no to good ideas for good reasons. People outside engineering trust your numbers.
Product Lead (after) Several products as one portfolio, and the coaching of Master Developers and Product Managers Your products fit together. You grow other people. You shape the vision with the head of the company.

What changes at each step

  • Master Developer to Product Manager: from planning the work to owning its results. You are judged on outcomes, not on plans.
  • Product Manager to Product Lead: from one product to a portfolio. You add the skill of growing other people.

Certificates and courses

Certificates and courses in the learning path support each step, but they do not replace the work.

A certificate shows you know the words. A defended roadmap shows you can do the job.

Free certificates and badges are welcome. Paid exams are optional and are listed separately in optional paid courses.

Key ideas

  • Own one, then own results, then own many.
  • Evidence moves you forward. A roadmap, then measured outcomes, then a portfolio.
  • Say no with reasons. This is the skill that separates a manager from a task-taker.
  • Grow others. At the top of the path, your product is your people.

Steps to plan your next step

  1. Write where you are today, in one sentence.
  2. Read the “ready” column for the next role.
  3. Pick the one item you can least prove today.
  4. Plan one piece of work that would prove it.
  5. Ask your Sensei to review the plan.

Working with AI agents

  • May: gather public descriptions of a role, summarise articles on product management, check that your plan has dates.
  • May not: decide your next role or write the evidence that proves you are ready.
  • A safe brief: “Summarise these three articles on product manager responsibilities in a table. Quote each source. Do not rank them.”

Common mistakes

  1. Collecting certificates instead of doing the work.
  2. Wanting the next title before the evidence.
  3. Never saying no.
  4. Ignoring finance and sales. People outside engineering must trust your numbers.
  5. Skipping coaching others. It starts early.

Practice

  1. Write the one-sentence difference between a Master Developer and a Product Manager.
  2. List three outcomes you could measure for your product.
  3. Say no to one good idea for one good reason, in writing.

Checklist

  • I can name the three roles and what each owns.
  • I know what evidence shows readiness.
  • I know certificates do not replace the work.
  • I have written my next step.

The 2030 Product Development Manager lens

  • You decide: what you aim at, and which evidence you will build.
  • You delegate to agents: research of role descriptions, drafting a plan table for you to edit, tracking dates.
  • A sample brief: “Turn my plan list into a dated table. Do not add items.”
  • You verify by: checking each date against your calendar and each claim against your work.
  • Disciplines involved: product, finance, sales, support, leadership and coaching.
  • On the agent ladder: the career path climbs the same ladder: use and delegate as a Master Developer, orchestrate as a Product Manager, govern as a Product Lead.
