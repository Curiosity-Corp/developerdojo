The Belts

Explore how evidence of field-specific ability can complement universal development ranks.

Show what you can develop

Practice a technique, adapt it, and show your work. The following belt stages are a practice framework for field-specific capability; Developer, Master Developer and Sensei remain the broader development and mentoring ranks.

  1. Practice

    Follow a documented technique and explain the goal, stack and checks.

  2. Adapt

    Change a constraint, tool or requirement and explain the effect on the outcome.

  3. Demonstrate

    Provide artifacts and evidence for independent review, including an unfamiliar challenge where appropriate.

Five stages of practice

Use these stages to see your next challenge. They are a proposed learning progression, not credentials that this website issues. A color does not replace independent review.

  1. White: See and explain

    Identify the outcome, its audience and a check for success. Explain what the harness, model and instructions each do.

  2. Yellow: Follow a kata

    Reproduce a documented result, record your stack and show that you checked it against the recipe.

  3. Green: Adapt the technique

    Change a requirement or tool, explain your decision and compare the result against the same checks.

  4. Brown: Solve an unfamiliar challenge

    Choose your own stack, respond to a failure or new constraint, and defend the evidence in a Randori exercise.

  5. Black: Improve and teach

    Combine or invent a repeatable technique, explain its limits, and help another learner reproduce and evaluate it.

Developer, Master Developer and Sensei are broader ranks. A Master Developer transfers judgment between tools or fields; a Sensei can explain, review and teach a technique. A field practice stage does not automatically confer either rank. See the existing rank and certificate process.

The shared skills travel across fields; the artifact, standards and checks change. These are starting points, not claims that every complete specialist pathway is available today.

Software and prototypes

Make: A working, editable prototype and an explanation of who it serves.

Verify: Test the main action, keyboard use and a narrow phone screen; record failures and repairs.

Randori twist: A key interaction must also work without a mouse.

SaaS UX and visual design

Make: A user journey and an editable screen or prototype with stated design choices.

Verify: Check the journey, readable text, contrast and a phone-sized layout with the intended audience in mind.

Randori twist: A user cannot complete the main task in the originally planned way.

3D, BIM and construction

Make: An editable practice model or design brief with units, assumptions and a review plan.

Verify: Check the inputs, dimensions and information against the source; identify what a qualified person must review.

Randori twist: The requested material, file format or design constraint changes.

Business and data

Make: A useful report or workflow using fictional or permissioned data, with a trace to its inputs.

Verify: Recalculate totals, check claims against source data and identify any action requiring approval.

Randori twist: The source has a duplicate or an important value is missing.

Robotics and manufacturing

Make: A simulation plan or design study that states its inputs, units and assumptions.

Verify: Review the simulated result against the task and identify the specialist checks needed before any physical use.

Randori twist: A required dimension or operating condition changes.

Science and health research

Make: A research question, evidence log and analysis using fictional, public or permissioned material.

Verify: Trace claims to sources, distinguish evidence from inference and record uncertainty; obtain qualified review for real health decisions.

Randori twist: A source contradicts the current conclusion.

Media and communications

Make: A draft script or visual with an intended audience, sources and usage permissions.

Verify: Check claims, accessibility and rights; compare the final output with the approved brief.

Randori twist: The output must work without sound or for a different audience.

Education and learning

For safety-critical or regulated work, practice artifacts are not professional approval. Get the applicable independent validation and qualified approval before real-world use.

Prepare an evidence review

  1. Bring the work: an artifact, the goal and the checks, the stack and the actual results, including failed attempts and limits.
  2. Explain and demonstrate: show what you and the agents did, how you checked it and why the work meets the criteria.
  3. Respond to a new situation: try an unfamiliar task or change a tool or constraint; explain and verify your choices.
  4. Get independent feedback: use a qualified reviewer where the field requires it. The existing Sensei review guide offers a process for a review, not a promise that one has been arranged.

Only share work you have permission to share. Do not put passwords, tokens, private customer information or confidential material in your checklist. A review, local tick or course completion does not automatically issue a belt or certificate.
