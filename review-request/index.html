Ranks · specialist belts · transfers · independent review

Ask for an independent review

See what a rank, a specialist belt or a transfer into a field asks for, check that your evidence covers it, and prepare a request you hand privately to the pilot coordinator. Nothing leaves your device.

Prepare your requestHow a review works

A request, not an application: A review request awards nothing and is not an application for a certificate. The rubrics it names are proposed, not approved, and no pilot review has run yet. A rank certificate still follows the certificate process, with a separate Maintainer deciding. Dojo assessment is not professional licensure or accreditation. This website takes no authenticated submissions: your request stays on your device until you copy it.

How an independent review works, end to end

Two people besides you take part: a pilot coordinator, who keeps the confidential record of the case, and an independent reviewer with relevant expertise and no conflict of interest. Nobody reviews their own work.

  1. Agree scope, access and consent. The learner and reviewer agree in writing what the reviewer may open, keep and share. Learner work stays private by default and is never given to an AI service without permission.
    You write the scope in your request on this page.
  2. Choose an independent reviewer. The reviewer has relevant expertise and no conflict of interest, and is a different person from whoever coordinates the pilot. Nobody reviews their own work.
  3. Send the evidence packet. The learner prepares the six kinds of evidence above in their own repository or files and gives the reviewer read access. The Dojo website receives nothing.
    You say where each kind of evidence is on this page.
  4. Set the unseen task. The reviewer, not the learner, chooses the unseen constraint, the changed tool or the injected failure, and reveals it only when the task starts. The learner works with it while the reviewer observes.
    You see its kind on this page, never the task.
  5. Record each criterion. The reviewer records every criterion as observed, not yet observed or unclear, with an evidence reference, then gives one strength and one gap. There is no score and no pass mark.
    The reviewer uses the review record.
  6. Keep the decision separate. The pilot issues no belt and no rank. A rank certificate still follows the existing issue, correct and revoke steps, with a separate Maintainer deciding.
  7. Report what the pilot learned. The coordinator writes a short report: what worked, what was unclear, how long it took and which rubric changes to propose to the program policy review. Nothing identifies the learner without their consent.

The reviewer records every criterion as observed, not yet observed or unclear, each with an evidence reference. There is no score and no pass mark. Not yet observed is a valid result that comes with a next step. See what each rank and belt asks.

Prepare your request

For the learner. What you type is saved only in this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere and there is no account. Do not type names, passwords or private material: say where the evidence is, and give the reviewer access there.

Your review request

Developer rank

Can this person develop one outcome from start to finish and show evidence that it works?

The unseen task. You will not see it in advance: the reviewer sets it and reveals it when the work starts. Its kind: One changed requirement in your own field, chosen by the reviewer and revealed when the session starts.

What the reviewer will look for

  • DEV-1 Outcome and checks. The learner states the audience, the intended result and the checks before building, and the artifact passes those checks when the reviewer runs them.
    Evidence: The actual artifacts · Tests and checks
  • DEV-2 Reproducible stack. The learner records the harness, model, instructions, tools, versions and permissions actually used, and the reviewer can repeat the main run from that record.
    Evidence: Reproducible configuration · The actual artifacts
  • DEV-3 Failures on the record. At least one real failure or wrong turn is recorded with what caused it, what changed and which check confirmed the repair.
    Evidence: Failures · Tests and checks · Review log

Master Developer rank

Can this person carry their judgment to a tool family or field they did not practice in?

The unseen task. You will not see it in advance: the reviewer sets it and reveals it when the work starts. Its kind: A brief in a tool family or field you did not practice, and a tool withdrawn or a failure injected while you work on it.

What the reviewer will look for

  • MD-1 Transfer to an unseen tool family or field. Given a brief the learner has not seen, in a tool family or field other than the one practiced, the learner chooses and adapts a stack, and the result passes checks the reviewer chose.
    Evidence: The actual artifacts · Reproducible configuration · Tests and checks · Decision evidence
  • MD-2 Recovery from a changed tool or failure. When the reviewer withdraws a tool or injects a failure during the task, the learner diagnoses it, changes the approach, repeats the same checks and records what changed.
    Evidence: Failures · Tests and checks · Review log
  • MD-3 Decisions defended without notes. In the review the learner explains each main decision, the alternative they rejected, which check proves what, and what is still unverified, and adapts the plan when the reviewer adds a constraint.
    Evidence: Decision evidence · Review log

Sensei rank

Can this person teach and review a technique that works in many fields, and show how it adapts in each?

The unseen task. You will not see it in advance: the reviewer sets it and reveals it when the work starts. Its kind: Teaching a technique to a second volunteer under a constraint the reviewer chooses, adapting it to a second field named on the day, and reviewing a packet with one known defect.

What the reviewer will look for

  • SEN-1 Teach a generalizable technique. Another learner reproduces the technique from the Sensei's explanation, then meets an unseen constraint the reviewer chose, and the Sensei coaches without doing the work for them.
    Evidence: The actual artifacts · Review log · Decision evidence
  • SEN-2 Domain adaptations. The Sensei shows how the technique changes in a second field the reviewer names: the different inputs, tools, checks and the qualified review that field requires.
    Evidence: Decision evidence · Reproducible configuration · Tests and checks
  • SEN-3 Independent review of another person's work. Given a packet they did not prepare, with a known defect added by the pilot coordinator, the Sensei records each criterion as observed, not yet observed or unclear, with evidence references, and finds the defect or says what stopped them.
    Evidence: Review log · Failures · Decision evidence
